The Trail Metaphor

A Map of Your Crew

A trail map shows you the terrain. But the best trips start with something else first: knowing who you're hiking with. A sociogram is that second map — one that surfaces the hidden connections already present in your classroom before anyone has spoken a word. Shared interests, parallel paths, overlapping goals, common questions. When students can see themselves reflected in their classmates, a course section becomes something more than a space they share. It becomes a community they belong to.

The UDL Connection

Belonging as an Access Issue

Traditional icebreakers can be high-stakes for students who experience social anxiety, process conversations differently, or feel pressure to perform connection in real time. A sociogram shifts that dynamic entirely.

A Gemini-generated sociogram infographic in Moodle:

  • Reduces cognitive load by making social patterns visible rather than requiring students to hold them mentally
  • Supports varied processing needs through visual, spatial, and reflective participation
  • Lowers social pressure compared to verbal or live icebreakers
  • Normalizes learner variability by highlighting both commonalities and diversity within the class
  • Fosters belonging by helping students recognize shared interests, goals, and experiences
  • Encourages peer connection by identifying possible affinity points for collaboration

Who benefits most: Students with social anxiety, ADHD, autism spectrum characteristics, and anyone for whom traditional introductions feel performative rather than connective.

Your Trail Activity

Map Your Class With Gemini

Post four beginning-of-course questions in Moodle, collect student responses, then use Gemini Canvas to generate a sociogram infographic that reveals the connections already in the room.

1
Post the forum in Moodle. Create a Forum activity and ask these four questions: (1) What is your name? (2) What are you studying? (3) What is something you care about deeply that might not appear on a résumé or transcript? (4) When you imagine yourself doing meaningful work in the future, what are you doing, who are you helping, or what problem are you addressing? Give students at least a day or two to respond.
2
Open Gemini Canvas and generate the sociogram. In Gemini, click the "+" menu and select Canvas to open the split-screen view. Paste the student responses into the sociogram prompt from the Click Guide below, then press Enter. Gemini will build the infographic in the Canvas panel on the right. Use the prompt and the canvas editing tools to refine it — adjust colors, labels, layout, or theme clusters until it feels right for your class.
3
Embed it in your Moodle course. Use the Share + Export button in Gemini Canvas to copy the HTML code. In Moodle, add a Page activity, open the source code editor in the Content section, and paste in the code. Save and display. If the layout doesn't render correctly, return to Gemini and ask it to fix the code for Moodle — then repeat with the new code.

🎒 Bonus: Review the sociogram yourself before sharing it with students — you may discover patterns and affinities in your class that will shape how you design the rest of your course. What clusters do you see? What surprises you?
